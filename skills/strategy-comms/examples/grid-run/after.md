# Recommendation: fund the fix to the three workflows behind 62% of support tickets

I recommend funding the workflow fix for Q2: two engineers for one quarter on invoice export, SSO setup and bulk import, at [cost of two engineers for one quarter]. I'm asking you to approve it on Friday, ahead of adding four support agents or building in-app help. Those three workflows produced 62% of our support tickets last quarter, and a model-judged comparison of the three options favours this one.

The main point against it is the Q2 enterprise renewals. Seven of the eleven enterprise accounts renewing in Q2 have open escalations older than 30 days, and on that condition the comparison judged adding agents the better answer and the workflow fix a weak one. [plan for the seven escalated accounts if the workflow fix is funded]

## The options and the facts they were judged against

I ran the three options through strategy-grid, in which a model judges each option against each condition separately. Every score below is that model's judgment of one pairing, not a measurement. The four conditions were:

1. 62% of support tickets last quarter came from three workflows: invoice export, SSO setup and bulk import.
2. Eleven enterprise accounts renew in Q2, and seven of them have open escalations older than 30 days. This is the one condition marked as pivotal.
3. Fewer than 1 in 10 customers who filed a ticket opened a help article in the session before filing.
4. Ticket volume rises about 40% in the six weeks after each quarterly release.

## Why the workflow fix

On the grid's read, the workflow fix is the only option the conditions favour overall: +0.31, against -0.11 for adding agents and -0.23 for in-app help. None of the four conditions fights it, and it was judged the best answer to two of them. On ticket concentration (+1.0, high confidence) it targets exactly the three workflows the 62% comes from, and if the fixes land the concentration itself goes away. On release spikes (+0.5, medium confidence) it reduces the spike at its source, if releases are what break or change these workflows. That makes it the only option judged to change two of the four conditions; the others change one each. Its overall score stays well short of +1 because the other two conditions barely bear on it: unread help (0.0) and the enterprise renewals (+0.25).

Each of the other options has a condition that fights it. For adding agents it is the release spikes (-0.5): fixed headcount is a poor fit for volume that rises 40% for six weeks a quarter, because staffed for the peak it idles and staffed for the average it falls behind, although four more agents would still shorten the queue during a spike compared with today. For in-app help it is unread help (-0.75): the option depends on customers reading help at the moment of trouble, and fewer than 1 in 10 open a help article before filing, although guidance inside the workflow may be read where help-center articles are not.

## What cuts against it

The strongest point against the workflow fix is the enterprise renewals, the one condition marked as pivotal, where adding agents was judged the better answer (+0.75 at high confidence, against +0.25 at medium confidence for the fix). The grid's reasoning is that the seven escalations are already open and older than 30 days, so fixing workflows this quarter does nothing to close them before the renewals; at most, fewer new tickets would free a little agent time late in the quarter. Adding agents addresses the escalations directly, because the hires would be trained on enterprise accounts first. The grid's own caveat on that is that new hires take time to become useful on enterprise escalations, and Q2 may be over before they are.

The fix also has no case without the first condition. If tickets were spread evenly across the product, fixing three workflows would have no case. The grid's caveat on its own top score is that the 62% says where tickets come from, not that the causes can be fixed in a quarter, and some of that volume may be setup questions no fix removes. In-app help rests on the same condition, so two of the three options stand or fall on that figure.

## What the comparison did not judge

The grid does not judge cost, so none of the scores reflects the cost line in the table.

It also judged that none of the three options pushes back on the third condition. That fewer than 1 in 10 customers open a help article before filing has no bearing on the first two options and fights the third.

Funding one option leaves part of the picture uncovered. As a set, the three options score 0.53 (band 0.47 to 0.59), against 0.31 for the workflow fix alone. In-app help adds nothing to that set score, because it is the best answer to no condition. So what the set has and the fix alone lacks is, on the grid's read, what adding agents does for the enterprise renewals.

## What would change the answer

The grid named the evidence each judgment turns on. Four of those bear on this decision:

- Whether the seven open escalations sit in the three workflows. Its read of the fix against the renewals turns on this.
- A breakdown by cause of the tickets from the three workflows, which is what the caveat on the 62% turns on.
- The ticket mix in the six weeks after a release. The condition does not say the post-release spike comes from these three workflows.
- How long a new agent takes to handle an enterprise escalation alone, which is what the case for adding agents before the renewals turns on.
