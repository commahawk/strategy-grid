# Where this lens comes from

The lens started as a study of one company's communication culture. Most of the company-specific sources below are public material about Capital One. They are combined with general methods that the lens pairs with them. The link between those methods and the company is an inference, and no public source confirms it. This is not an official Capital One writing standard, none was found in public sources, and nothing here is affiliated with or endorsed by the company. Its name appears in this file because honest sourcing needs it, and nowhere else in the skill. Read this when the user asks why a rule exists or how well supported it is.

How to read the labels:

- **Company page:** a page Capital One published. Most are recruiting pages or single-author Tech blog posts, so they show what the company selects for and praises, not a written policy.
- **Leader:** a named current or former Capital One leader in their own words, often about one organization or their personal practice.
- **Employee:** a current or former Capital One employee's personal blog or document, describing one person's or one team's practice.
- **Framework:** a general method. Public sources do not confirm it as the company's house practice.
- **Critique:** a published criticism, used here as a guard-rail.
- **Counter-example:** public Capital One material that points the other way from the rule.

Quotes below were matched word for word by a separate verification pass (November 2026) against the cited page or, where noted, a reproduction of it. Exceptions are marked.

## Main point first (question 1)

- Company page, Strategy interviewing page: "You should lead with your answer recommending the best path, then support that answer with evidence." <https://www.capitalonecareers.com/strategy-interviewing>
- Company page, case interview tips: "Your answer should take the form of a recommendation about what steps the business owner should take and why." <https://www.capitalonecareers.com/4-tips-to-ace-your-capital-one-case-interview-101-students>
- Scope: both are interview guidance for candidates. No public source was found that describes how recommendations are presented internally.
- Framework, Minto's Pyramid Principle (via Will Larson's notes on the book): "ideas at any level in the pyramid must always be summaries of the ideas grouped below them". <https://lethain.com/pyramid-principle/>
- Counter-example: in the public samples found, leaders set context first or advise doing so. Rich Fairbank's published note on hybrid work (18 May 2022, about 1,400 words) spends roughly 255 words on shared context before its one-sentence dated decision. <https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/reopen-hybrid-work/> On the Q3 2025 earnings call he says "let me just pull up and give context to the whole thing." (one machine-assisted transcript) <https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/10/21/capital-one-cof-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript> Jay Michelini, VP of product at Capital One Business, on announcing change: "You’ve got to lead with the ‘why,’" (PYMNTS, 2026). <https://www.pymnts.com/dashboard-post/news/banking/2026/capital-one-urges-leaders-to-listen-before-they-launch/> The skill is deliberately stricter than these, with the main point inside the first three sentences.
- The allowance for up to two sentences of setup rests on the Strategy interviewing page ("Often, it helps to repeat what you're solving for back to the interviewer to make sure you're on the same page.") and on a practitioner critique of answer-first writing that was collected but not fact-checked (see "What could not be verified").

## An explicit ask and a named decider (question 2)

- This rule is a corrective, not a description. No public Capital One source asks for a single named decider, and the company's own page says: "Teamwork is at the heart of everything we do and decisions are made by wisdom of the crowd." <https://www.capitalonecareers.com/how-we-work> The memo shape's "who was consulted, and where they differ" is the part that reflects that line.
- Framework, Bain's RAPID: "Ideally, there should only be one decider for each decision." <https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-tool-to-clarify-decision-accountability/>
- Leader (co-founder Nigel Morris, describing the one-page board update he asks portfolio CEOs for at QED Investors, which is not a Capital One format): "it's a one-pager that says: (1) what's going really well, (2) what's not going well, (3) what's keeping you up at night", plus the decisions needed today. <https://fintechleaders.substack.com/p/nigel-morris-qed-and-capital-one>
- Leader (Morris, his own principle for comparing ideas in one unit, which sits behind "options in comparable terms"): "NPV is the real McCoy, and that's how you should do it." <https://fintechleaders.substack.com/p/nigel-morris-qed-and-capital-one-be9>
- Critique: one anonymous review on Blind (a current data analyst, October 2026): "Decisions move slowly and often require alignment across multiple layers." It appeared in a rotating list beneath a different review at <https://www.teamblind.com/company/Capital-One/review/QwhLgvwXi0>, could be read only through a tool that extracts page text, and may not stay there. Refusing "looking for alignment" as an ask is this lens's inference. The review does not mention documents.

## Facts first, the read marked as a read (question 3)

- Leader (Nigel Morris, 2024, speaking as managing partner of QED Investors about his own leadership values): "I want to go to facts first and then I can add art and intuition on top of that." And his model sentence: "Look, I'm not sure if I'm right, but here's the pattern of facts I see, and I think it means this." <https://www.shrm.org/executive-network/insights/truth-loyalty-empathy-leadership-capital-one-founder-morris>
- Company page, the "Intellectually Rigorous" value: "We look to every associate to exercise judgement, challenge conventional thinking, and make fact-based decisions." The page names fact-based decisions and judgment as values. It says nothing about keeping them apart or marking them. <https://www.capitalonecareers.com/how-we-work>
- Leader (Catherine McGarvey, SVP Developer Experience, 2025, describing how her own organization works; the profile does not discuss memos, documents, decks or email): "If we can't clearly tell whether we've made something better, we probably haven't." <https://www.capitalone.com/tech/culture/meet-catherine-mcgarvey/>
- Company page, on showing method: "communicate your approach to your interviewer before proceeding with calculations." (Strategy interviewing page)
- Framework, US intelligence community directive ICD 203 (2015), tradecraft standard 3. In this lens's judgment it is the clearest public statement of the rule, and it has no connection to Capital One: "Analytic products should clearly distinguish statements that convey underlying intelligence information used in analysis from statements that convey assumptions or judgments." <https://archive.dni.gov/files/documents/ICD/ICD-203.pdf>

## Numbers into decisions, outcomes over activity (question 4)

- Company page (a recruiter, on the business analyst interview page): "We want to see if you can turn numbers into business decisions and do it in a collaborative way." <https://www.capitalonecareers.com/en/cracking-the-business-analyst-interview-students-101-cdev>
- Company page (a UK Director of Strategy and Analytics, in a recruiting profile): "We can have the most brilliant analysis in the world, but if we can't tell a compelling story about why it matters and what needs to change, it won't be implemented." <https://jobs.capitalone.co.uk/meet-the-strategy-analysts>
- Leader (McGarvey, same profile, about the Developer Experience organization): "we shifted from “we’re shipping a feature” to “we’re enabling developers to deploy with confidence.”"
- Leader (Fairbank in Knowledge at Wharton, 2004, quoting what Signet Bank's CFO told him in a 1991 budget meeting, before Capital One was spun off): "I can't find one dime of that eloquence in our financial statements." The page could be read only through a tool that extracts page text. <https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/when-leadership-becomes-a-quest>

## Risks and contrary evidence (question 5)

- Company page: a strong recommendation includes "A list of risks/concerns with potential solutions." and "forward-looking considerations ... such as further analyses needed". (Strategy interviewing page) The page pairs risks with solutions. It does not say to put them near the top. That placement is this lens's choice, for the reasons in the two critiques below.
- Leader (Fairbank at a 2015 Barclays conference, as quoted by The Motley Fool, explaining rising losses): "This is all growth math. This is not a worsening of the consumer." In this one passage he gives the mechanism and then says what the number does not mean. The skill adopts the move only as far as the draft's own evidence supports it, because a reader discounts a reassuring explanation when the cause is not shown. <https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2015/09/22/3-things-capital-ones-ceo-richard-fairbank-wants-i.aspx>
- Leader (Fairbank, 2005): "We mechanically plug into all our decisions a significant worsening of the economy and of the competitive marketplace." <https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btghbolxjdxnr1f63xts/home/richard-fairbank-of-capital-one-financial-corp-card-sharp>
- Leader (Morris, 2024, on the method in Capital One's early years): "In classic Karl Popper language, you would look to try to disprove the hypothesis." <https://fintechleaders.substack.com/p/nigel-morris-qed-and-capital-one-be9> And, on his own practice: "Strategy is a dynamic process written in pencil." <https://excoleadership.com/?p=17366>
- Framework, ICD 203 standard 6: "Products should be internally consistent and acknowledge significant supporting and contrary information affecting judgments."
- Critique, the OCC's 2020 consent order following the cybersecurity breach (findings the bank neither admitted nor denied): "Internal audit also did not effectively report on and highlight identified weaknesses and gaps to the Audit Committee." This lens's reading is that a risk which has been identified but is not made prominent to the people who must act is the failure this question guards against. <https://www.occ.gov/static/enforcement-actions/ea2020-037.pdf>
- Critique, the Columbia Accident Investigation Board, via Edward Tufte: "The choice of headings, arrangement of information, and size of bullets on the key chart served to highlight what management already believed." <https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/columbia-accident-investigation-board-the-boeing-powerpoint-slide/>

## Plain and short (question 6)

- Leader (McGarvey, same profile, about the Developer Experience organization): "Writing things down concisely, aligning on principles before jumping into debate".
- Company page, case interview tips: explain it "to someone who works in a totally different field."
- Employee (Jamie Tanna's personal blog, 2018; the post is tagged #capital-one but does not name the company, and is about explaining things in conversation): "What would I want to know if I'd just walked into the room and had no context?" <https://jvt.me/posts/2018/08/16/context-is-king>
- Employee (Tanna, 2021, on one team's chat habits): "make sure that you're actually providing someone with the context they need to reply to you". <https://jvt.me/posts/2021/11/05/secret-sauce>
- Leader (head of AI design, 2018, on customer-facing writing): "We've actually trained our customers to be super rigid and now they think they have to talk bankspeak." <https://digiday.com/marketing/weve-trained-customers-super-rigid-lessons-capital-ones-chatbot-eno>
- Critique (Richard Bove of Odeon Capital Group on Fairbank's public style, Bank Director, 2022): "He's got this very slow way, highly repetitious method of communicating". An illustration of why the skill says to say each thing once. <https://www.bankdirector.com/article/capital-ones-secret-to-success/>
- Framework, Amazon's narrative memo (Bezos, 2017 shareholder letter), which this lens treats as the main alternative to the consulting deck: "We don’t do PowerPoint ... Instead, we write narratively structured six-page memos." No public evidence was found that Capital One uses this format. <https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2017-letter-to-shareholders>

## Structure fitted to the problem (question 6)

- Company page: "We don't use standard frameworks in our own projects, so we want to see that you can use your creativity and judgment". The same page asks that "Factors should be provided in buckets to create a Mutually Exclusive & Collectively Exhaustive (MECE) framework." and tells candidates to "make sure the considerations you're providing are distinct from each other." (Strategy interviewing page)
- Framework, Minto on lead-ins that only count (via StrategyU's review of the book): "Most writers simply group ideas, without completing the thinking." <https://strategyu.co/pyramid-principle-2/>
- Framework, takeaway titles (Slideworks): "The goal is to be able to read the titles and from that alone understand the gist of the deck." <https://slideworks.io/resources/how-to-write-action-titles-like-mckinsey>
- On three supporting points: one consulting-prep source presents three as a digestibility aid: "Groups of threes are often the key to make information easily digestible." <https://www.myconsultingcoach.com/news/communicate-like-a-consultant-the-rule-of-three> That three is a convenience and not a property of the content is this lens's inference. The "main point, three pillars, evidence" structure this skill started from is said to come from a Medium post by a former Capital One product manager. The post could not be opened, so neither its contents nor that description of its author is verified.

## Challenge, voiced directly (critique mode, and the CLAUDE.md block)

- Leader (Morris, 2024, same SHRM interview, on his own values): "I want everybody in the room to be willing to challenge me and be willing to speak truth to power, particularly in the interpretation of facts." <https://www.shrm.org/executive-network/insights/truth-loyalty-empathy-leadership-capital-one-founder-morris>
- Company page (a tech leader's first-person essay on the careers site): "The most exciting part of a technical discussion for me is being proven wrong by data." <https://www.capitalonecareers.com/system-and-soul-balancing-an-ai-career-tech-tlead-cdev>
- Employee (Matthew Reinbold's personal "manager README", 2019; personal, not policy): "I ask for your input, not your fealty." <https://matthewreinbold.com/2019/02/14/AManagerREADME>
- Leader (Melanie Frank, quoted on the Tech blog), on feedback: "Give specific, actionable, behavior-based feedback." <https://www.capitalone.com/tech/culture/wit-supporting-the-next-generation/>

## Process and document formats (the shapes)

- Company page (a sample day on the Strategy Consulting recruiting page; illustrative, not a stated process): the associate who drove the analysis leads a team problem-solving session, takes the team's feedback, and then starts "building slides based on your analysis to communicate your recommendations to business partners." <https://www.capitalonecareers.com/strategy-experience>
- Company page (a 2023 Tech blog post by one author): Distinguished Engineers use a Request for Comment process that "enables DE's across the enterprise to ask questions and get feedback in a simplified and offline way." The page says "offline" and that comments are captured in Jira. <https://capitalone.com/tech/culture/continued-development-senior-ic-engineers>
- Company page (one engineer's 2023 Tech blog post, conditional on team norms): "If the PR author is responsible for solely testing the changes, screenshots should be added to the PR description to show the tests that were run along with the successful completion of those tests." <https://www.capitalone.com/tech/software-engineering/pull-request-best-practices/>
- Decision record: the "architecture decision description" template by Jeff Tyree and Art Akerman of Capital One (IEEE Software, 2005; matched against a third-party reproduction at <https://github.com/joelparkerhenderson/architecture-decision-record/blob/main/locales/en-001/templates/decision-record-template-by-jeff-tyree-and-art-akerman/index.md>) lists 14 fields, beginning issue, decision, status, group, assumptions, constraints, positions (the options considered), argument, implications. Its first field: "Describe the architectural design issue you’re addressing, leaving no questions about why you’re addressing this issue now." The decision-memo shape borrows its opening (the issue and why now) and its options, argument and implications fields, and reorders the rest. Decider, date, risks and who was consulted are additions. It is a published paper by two Capital One architects. Slab's template library republishes it as Capital One's and says the company's tech team uses it, but that wording is Slab's, and no Capital One source shows that it was or is a house format.
- The four-box status update is the one-page board update co-founder Nigel Morris asks portfolio CEOs for at QED Investors (see question 2 above). It is not a Capital One format.
- Announcing a decision: the elements come from one published sample, Rich Fairbank's 2022 hybrid-work note, which has shared context, the decision in one dated sentence, the condition that would delay it, the reasons on both sides, and an approach he calls "initial". It is one note, not a stated format, and the shape here puts the decision first where the note puts context first. <https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/reopen-hybrid-work/>

## What could not be verified

- **A Medium post said to be by a former Capital One product manager** (main point, three pillars, storyline before deck, "so what?"). The page could not be opened, so its contents and its authorship are unverified. Working out the analysis before building slides is consistent with the sample day on the Strategy Consulting page above, though that page does not use the word storyline. The rest is unverified.
- **A Glassdoor review titled "Consultant culture".** The page could not be opened. A search-result summary reports a complaint that decks are emphasized over complete sentences. Nothing is quoted and nothing here relies on it.
- **The Meeting Playbook.** Capital One's careers site confirms it exists (a deliverable of the 2021 Meeting Management program, which aimed to raise meeting quality and cut meeting quantity). Its contents are not public.
- **Conventions for weekly updates, decision memos and deck templates.** No public source describing them was found. The shapes in this skill are built from the principles above and general practice.
- **A practitioner critique of the Pyramid Principle (Stratrix).** It is the source of two ideas in the skill: that clean structure can look more certain than the analysis, and that with a hostile audience you agree the question before giving the answer. It was collected but not fact-checked, so it is not quoted.
